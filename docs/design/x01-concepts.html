CONCEPT 1

Big Keys

Input-first. The pad owns the screen and the scores sit on top.

Rationale

  • Lowest mis-tap risk. Keys are 60 px on the phone and about 110 px on the tablet, and they take half the screen. Showing values on the keys (T18 shows 54) helps kids add up.
  • You pick the modifier first and it resets to Single after each dart, so the common case takes one tap. Triple disables Bull, so an impossible dart can't be entered.
  • The other scores are only chips. That works for 2–4 players, but 5–6 need two rows of chips or a horizontal scroll, and they can't be read from across the room.
  • The remaining score is large (104 px on the phone) but shares the screen with the pad. The thrower can read it from 2–3 m. Others in the room have a harder time.
  • Teams: a chip shows the team score with the current thrower's name under it. Portrait and landscape use the same components in a different arrangement.
CONCEPT 2

Living Room

Scoreboard-first: a TV-style board readable from the sofa, with the pad in one low band.

Rationale

  • Built for a tablet on the wall. The 158 px numbers can be read from 3–4 m, and everyone sees every score, like a pub TV board.
  • The two rows of numbers (1–10, 11–20) read like a number line, so younger kids find numbers without scanning a grid.
  • The keys are the narrowest of the four: about 76×100 px on the tablet and exactly 56×56 px on the phone. That meets the minimum, but this concept has the highest mis-tap risk on a phone.
  • Up to 4 columns fit well. 5–6 players need a second row of cards or smaller numbers. Teams fit naturally as columns or cards with the members' names.
  • The phone portrait version is a fallback. The concept works best in landscape on a mounted tablet.
CONCEPT 3

Turn Lane

The three darts are the hero. The checkout route fills the empty slots, and you choose the number before the ring.

Rationale

  • The turn is the story. Each slot shows the score left after that dart, and the empty slots preview the checkout, so kids "fill in" T18 then D25 instead of doing the maths.
  • Number first, then ring, with a big sheet that spells out the points (Triple = 54). Nothing has to be armed in advance, so a forgotten Double or Triple can't cause a wrong entry.
  • Bust is unmistakable: a red dart, the struck-through 44 next to 124, and a locked pad until someone confirms.
  • A scoring dart takes two taps, which is slower for quick adult players. Possible fix: a long press enters a single, or an option skips the sheet for single-only numbers.
  • The light theme suits a bright room. The lane is tall, so landscape would put the lane and pad side by side. With 6 players the tab row scrolls. Team tabs would show the team score with the thrower's name.
CONCEPT 4

Thumb Rail

One-handed compact phone with the ring rail on the side. On a tablet the pad splits to both edges.

Rationale

  • Every input is within thumb reach in the bottom half of the phone. The S/D/T rail can be moved to either side for left-handers, and its tall keys (about 104 px) are easy to hit.
  • On the tablet the controls hug both edges, leaving a large central board. This suits two hands, a mounted tablet, or two kids helping each other.
  • The 4-column grid (1–4, 5–8…) is less familiar than 5 columns, and the rail costs width, so the phone number keys are about 65×60 px.
  • Other players appear in the smallest form of the four (ticker pills). The pills scroll, so 6 players or teams fit, but they can't be read at a distance on a phone.
  • Teams: a pill shows the team score with the thrower's initials. The ladder on the tablet lists both members under each team.
